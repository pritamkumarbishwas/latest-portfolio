## Problem

The client's legacy storefront coupled content, catalog, and checkout into one
monolithic app. Merchandisers could not ship a landing page without a deploy, page
loads hovered around four seconds on mobile, and every seasonal campaign added
another round of performance regressions.

Checkout was the worst offender: a third-party script bundle blocked rendering on
the one page that directly influenced revenue.

## Approach

I separated the system into three composable layers:

1. **Content and catalog** were modeled as APIs with typed clients, so pages could
   be composed per route instead of rendered from a single template.
2. **Product pages rendered at the edge**, cached by SKU with tag-based
   invalidation when inventory or pricing changed.
3. **Checkout moved to a minimal Stripe flow** with no blocking third-party tags
   until after payment confirmation.

The design system carried the visual consistency — the same tokens and components
used in marketing pages powered the store, so accessibility fixes landed once.

```ts
const product = await getProduct(sku, {
  next: { tags: [`product:${sku}`], revalidate: 300 },
});
```

<Callout type="note">
  Tag-based revalidation meant a price change invalidated exactly one product
  page — not the entire catalog cache.
</Callout>

## Stack

- **React** and TypeScript for storefront components
- **Stripe** for payments with a tokenized, PCI-friendly checkout
- **Edge caching** with tag-based revalidation for catalog freshness
- **Playwright** coverage for the purchase path

## Results

- Largest Contentful Paint improved from **4.1s to 1.3s** on mid-tier mobile
- Conversion rate rose **18%** in the first month after launch
- Merchandisers shipped campaign pages in hours with no engineering deploy
